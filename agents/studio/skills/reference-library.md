# Skill: Reference library (Same Work, approved v1, Oct 19, 2026)

What was researched and approved for the Same Work kit on Oct 19, 2026: the pages we learn from, the looks, the gear and the edit suite, with their sources. Use it two ways:

- **Building a new kit:** start from these references and this gear baseline when the brief asks for the same kind of thing (a real-feeling athlete documentary for Instagram and TikTok). Re-check anything with a date before you repeat it, and carry each source into the new kit's Sources card.
- **Refreshing Same Work:** this is what the approved kit says. A refresh run also has the approved kit itself (kit_overview, kit_read_column), and the kit wins if the two ever differ.

Every fact below has a date. Gear lists, prices, approval lists and follower counts go stale: re-check them before they go into a kit, and write the new date.

## The feel

_The pages we learn from: what to take and what to leave._

### The vision

Same Work should feel like MADE Athletes' honesty, opened like a Players' Tribune story, shot like a Mark Bone film and packaged for the feed the way Overtime does it.

Parent stories borrow Humans of New York's stillness. On the Mic borrows UNINTERRUPTED's sit-down, where athletes say what they actually think.

### Humans of New York

What it is: one person, one photo, their words. About 12.6 million followers.

Take this: no text on the photo. Natural light, a real place, nobody posed. The caption starts mid-thought, in the first person, with no adjectives from us.

Use it for: parent cards and parent stories.

Leave this: nothing. It's the cleanest model we have.

Link: https://www.instagram.com/humansofny/

### The Players' Tribune

What it is: first-person athlete stories, "The voice of the game."

Take this: the headline is the athlete's own line in a big serif, often with one word in italic. A short quote sits under it and a small caps credit sits bottom right. First lines drop you into a moment: "Training camp, 2009. My rookie year."

Use it for: In Their Own Words quote cards and letters.

Leave this: long essays in the caption. Our captions stay under 60 words; a letter goes on the slides.

Link: https://www.instagram.com/playerstribune/

### MADE Athletes

What it is: a "mentorship-first" athlete development and NIL agency (madeathletes.com (https://madeathletes.com/)): "Selective roster · Intentional support · No hype."

Take this: documentary frames with a muted, warm grade. Wide-tracked condensed caps across the frame ("Build the athlete behind the athlete"). Landscape openers and black title cards. Their Samoa film (https://www.instagram.com/made.athletes/reel/Da34AWXPXE8/) opens on a hard fact, then lets a coach and a mom talk. Manifesto: "Legacy is built, not given." (https://www.instagram.com/made.athletes/reel/DcgzUViRgNv/)

Use it for: title cards and the grade.

Leave this: betting-style posts on a high schooler's stats (this one (https://www.instagram.com/made.athletes/p/DeKunUrErvZ/)), and full names and tags on minors.

Link: https://www.instagram.com/made.athletes/

### UNINTERRUPTED: the Oct 16 reel

What it is: an UNINTERRUPTED Weekly clip. A host sits on the show set at a mic and gives a strong opinion, cut with game footage. Captions are bold, one line at a time. The post ends on a question ("team Go Dawgs or team Roll Tide?"), so the comments carry the argument. The account (@uninterrupted (https://www.instagram.com/uninterrupted/)) pins its named series to the profile.

Take this: the set, the mic, a real opinion, and a question people want to answer. Named series a parent can binge.

Use it for: On the Mic.

Leave this: broadcast footage and team logos (rule 4), and any take that runs down another athlete.

Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/DekBItesvN2/

### Mark Bone

What it is: a documentary director who plans every shoot in Milanote.

Take this: the craft. Want, obstacle and change; first and last frames that rhyme; shooting rules; cinematic shots you earn.

Leave this: long runtimes. Our hero films run 60 to 90 seconds.

Link: https://www.youtube.com/@markbone

### Overtime

What it is: youth and high school sports media built for vertical feeds.

Take this: the mechanics. The payoff in the first second, the athlete tagged once cleared, the footage source credited ("via @…"), captions of one line.

Leave this: the roasting and the slang. That isn't the Coached by Hooks voice.

Link: https://www.instagram.com/overtime/

### What it adds up to

• Open with a fact or a moment, never a logo. "He ran this drill in 2017. He runs it on Sundays now."

• Let the person talk. No narrator unless it's Hooks, and no music louder than the voice.

• One story per film, told in the speaker's own words, cut to a point.

• Look like a film, behave like a post: cinematic frames, vertical first, captions burned in, the athlete tagged once they've signed a release.

• Their words, their way. Athletes write or approve every word we publish as theirs.

## The look and sound

_One look, one sound, one type style across every film. Change them only between seasons._

### Shooting rules

• Framing: interviews on one camera, the speaker a little off center, eyes in the top third. Close-ups on hands, cleats and eyes. At least one extreme wide of every field or gym.

• Movement: interviews on a tripod. Training handheld but steady; the camera shakes only during a live rep, matching the athlete's effort.

• Character presence: adults appear full face. Texas youth athletes appear only as hands, cleats, backs and silhouettes until counsel clears it.

• Payoff: every film ends on a frame that rhymes with its first: the same drill, years apart.

• Frame for vertical. Shoot horizontal, but keep the subject inside the middle third so a 9:16 crop works.

• Real time over slow motion. One slow-motion hero rep per film at most.

• Available light first. Interviews by a window or in shade, on the sandwich schedule: B-roll, interview, more B-roll.

### Color: the base look

• Naturalistic: true skin, restrained saturation, warm light where it exists. It suits a campaign built on an honest read.

• Grade global to granular: exposure, white balance, contrast, then saturation. Use subtractive saturation, not the slider.

• Leave archive footage its age. A light correction and a year label. The gap between then and now is the point.

### Vintage looks: three ways to get them

1. In camera, on an approved camera. Vintage glass on an adapter (a Helios 44-2 58mm, Canon FD or Super Takumar primes) and a light diffusion filter (Black Pro-Mist 1/8 or 1/4). This still counts toward Netflix's 90%.
2. Real old formats, inside the 10%. Super 8 film, a MiniDV or Hi8 camcorder, and 35mm stills (Portra 400 for color, Tri-X 400 for black and white) for parent portraits.
3. In the grade. DaVinci Resolve Studio's Film Look Creator (halation, bloom, grain, gate weave, film gate, flicker; Studio only), or the Dehancer or FilmConvert plugins. Grain goes on last.

### The honesty rule for vintage looks (locked rule card)

A vintage look never makes new footage pass for old.

• Year labels are always true. Archive keeps its real age.

• A "vintage" shot from this year never sits in the "then" half of a Same Drill.

• A camcorder date stamp shows the real date, or nothing.

It's rule 1 applied to pictures: real moments only.

### Sound

• The voice is the product. A lav plus a backup recorder, audio monitored on headphones, and 30 seconds of room tone at every location.

• Music sits under the voice, never over it. Choose the right song, then go find another one.

• Licensed music only, or each app's commercial library. Coached by Hooks is a business account, so trending songs may not be cleared for it.

### Type and on-screen text

• Captions: one font, white, with a soft shadow. Burned in, verbatim, never more than two lines.

• Keep text clear of the app's buttons: out of the bottom fifth and away from the right edge.

• Year labels on every archive clip, small, in the top corner.

• The end card runs 2 seconds: the Coached by Hooks mark only. No Ground Floor Sports, TOJ or Subject marks on screen.

• No hype words, and none of the banned list, in any on-screen text or caption.

## Gear

_Cameras from Netflix's 2026 approved list, plus everything around them._

### What "Netflix approved" means

Netflix's capture floor (Lensrentals, February 2026):

• At least 3,840 photosites wide.

• Raw, or an intra-frame codec at 4:2:2 or better.

• 10-bit or more, and at least 240 Mbps at 24fps.

• A log curve (S-Log3, V-Log, Log C and the like) and timecode.

At least 90% of a show's runtime comes from an approved camera. The other 10% can be drones, crash cams and other specialty cameras (Shotkit, updated January 2026).

Why it matters for us: Instagram doesn't need it, but it's a quality floor, and footage shot this way can go into a longer series later. Check Netflix's own list before buying; it changes.

### Cameras

Recommended pair: a Sony FX6 for interviews (full frame, built-in variable ND) and a Sony FX3 for training B-roll and handheld. Same S-Log3, one LUT, one look.

Other approved bodies at the same level: Canon EOS C70, C80 and R5 C; Panasonic S1H and BS1H; RED Komodo 6K.

Rent for a hero day: ARRI Alexa 35 or Alexa Mini LF; Sony Venice 2 or Burano.

Settings: log, 10-bit 4:2:2 or raw, UHD or above, 23.98 or 24fps.

### Lenses and filters

• A 24–70mm f/2.8 zoom for most of the day.

• A 35mm and an 85mm prime for interviews.

• One or two vintage primes on an adapter for the look: a Helios 44-2 58mm, a Canon FD 50mm f/1.4.

• A variable ND for the FX3. The FX6 has one built in.

• Black Pro-Mist 1/8 or 1/4 for softer highlights.

### Audio

• A wireless lav kit that also records inside the transmitter, so the backup recorder is built in. The Rode Wireless PRO records 32-bit float on board.

• A shotgun mic on camera for room sound.

• A 32-bit float field recorder for sit-downs, such as the Zoom F3.

• Closed-back headphones, such as the Sony MDR-7506.

• Thirty seconds of room tone at every location.

### Light and grip

• Available light first.

• One soft key for interviews: a 300W-class LED in a large softbox.

• A 5-in-1 reflector and a black flag for negative fill.

• A tripod with a fluid head for every interview.

• A shoulder rig or top handle for steady handheld, and sandbags.

### Vintage capture (the 10%)

• Super 8: Kodak's Super 8 camera ($5,495 MSRP at launch; the film scanner is sold separately), or a used Super 8 camera plus a lab that processes and scans.

• Tape: a MiniDV or Hi8 camcorder.

• Stills: a 35mm film camera for parent portraits.

• Behind the scenes: a phone, shot vertical.

Every one of these follows the clearance card, the same as any camera.

### Media, backup and drones

• Fast cards and two SSDs per shoot. Copy twice before you leave, in folders named by athlete ID.

• Drones: commercial flights need an FAA Part 107 certificate, and we never fly over people. The DJI Inspire 3 is the drone on Netflix's list.

## Edit suite

_Software, look tools, settings, and which tools may touch Same Work footage._

### Software

• DaVinci Resolve Studio: edit, color, Fairlight audio, local speech-to-text and subtitles. Studio is needed for Film Look Creator.

• Adobe Premiere Pro and After Effects: build each graphics template once as an Essential Graphics template (MOGRT), so the editor only swaps the words.

• Final Cut Pro: local captions, if that station is in use.

• Photoshop or Figma: stills and carousel slides.

### Look tools

• Film Look Creator in Resolve Studio: film-style color with halation, bloom, grain, gate weave, film gate and flicker.

• Dehancer Pro or FilmConvert Nitrate: film stock emulation plugins for Resolve and Premiere.

All three process on the editor's machine. No footage leaves it.

### Color and sound settings

• Color management first: timeline in DaVinci Wide Gamut, output Rec.709 Gamma 2.4.

• Grade in this order: base look, exposure, white balance (printer lights), contrast, subtractive saturation, black level, grain last. Save the season's look as one PowerGrade and adjust per shot. Never drop a LUT on everything and walk away.

• Archive gets a light correction and a year label, nothing more.

• Mix the dialogue first. Voice clearly above music, room tone in every gap, about −14 LUFS.

• Licensed music only. Keep the license file in the project folder.

### Approved tools (draft, Kyron re-approves each quarter)

Transcription:

• DaVinci Resolve speech-to-text. Local. Version 18.5 or newer.

• Adobe Premiere Pro speech-to-text. Confirm Content Analysis is off and save a screenshot each quarter.

• Rev human transcription, only with a signed Rev Enterprise agreement. Not Rev AI.

Captions:

• DaVinci Resolve subtitle track. Preferred for minor footage. Burned in or SRT.

• Adobe Premiere Pro captions. Hand-check the text before burn-in.

• Final Cut Pro captions. Local.

Governed by STL agreement Section 9: no tool may train on Hooks footage, and no minor footage goes to a cloud tool whose training opt-out hasn't been checked and saved this quarter.

### Not approved for Same Work footage (locked rule card)

• CapCut and any ByteDance tool. No minor footage, ever.

• Otter.ai. Trains on uploaded audio by default.

• Submagic, Opus Clip, AutoCap, VEED, Kapwing and other cloud auto-caption or auto-clip tools, until the producer reviews each one's terms.

• Any generative or face-aware "enhancement": AI upscaling that redraws faces, beauty filters, voice clones, generated B-roll, auto-reframe that crops around a face.

• Any free online transcription tool without a published no-training policy.

If a tool isn't on either list: ask the producer before uploading anything. The producer checks the terms, confirms the opt-out, saves a screenshot and adds the tool to one list with a one-line reason.

### Review and delivery

• Name files SW_[lane]_[athleteID]_[format]_[ratio]_v[##]_[YYYYMMDD], for example SW_YTH_A0142_HERO_9x16_v03_20261017.mp4.

• Export 1080×1920 for 9:16 and 1080×1350 for 4:5. Check each app's current limits before delivery.

• Deliver only to the producer's folder. No public links, no personal drives.

• Review copies go to the speaker through the producer, marked REVIEW COPY, on a link that expires. For a minor, the parent approves.

• Frame.io would suit review links, but Kyron checks its terms and adds it to the approved list first.
