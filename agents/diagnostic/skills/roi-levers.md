# The Three ROI Levers

An AI/automation opportunity is only worth recommending if it pulls at least one of these three levers. This is the qualification gate for every opportunity, every tool, every offer.

## The levers (in priority order)

### 1. Make more money — effectiveness
The change causes more revenue: more leads captured, more deals closed, faster follow-up, higher conversion, better upsell. This is the strongest lever because it's the easiest ROI to defend on the review call.

### 2. Save time — efficiency
The change gives hours back per week. Quantify it: hours × the owner's hourly value × 52. A task that eats 5 hrs/week at a $100/hr owner-value is a $26,000/yr line item — a $60/mo tool that removes 4 of those hours is not a cost, it's arbitrage.

### 3. Increase quality — of the product or service
The change makes the output or the client experience measurably better: fewer errors, faster response, more consistent, more personalized. Quality compounds into retention and referrals even when it doesn't show up on this month's P&L.

## The rule

**One of these three, or it's not worth our time.** If you can't name the lever an opportunity pulls, you can't recommend it. "It's a cool tool" is not a lever.

Two levers on one opportunity is a headline. The custom-GPT-for-a-business-listing example pulled two at once:
- Efficiency: the seller answered **zero** of the 400-500 repetitive buyer emails.
- Quality: buyers called it "the best experience we've had buying a business."

That double-lever is what justified a $3K+ knowledge-system build.

## How the levers map to the report

- Levers 1 and 3 (money, quality) anchor the **Executive Summary** — outcomes owners feel.
- Lever 2 (time) anchors the **Financial Impact** slide — the hours-reclaimed math.
- Every tool in the **Priority Matrix** carries its lever tag so the client sees *why*, not just *what*.
