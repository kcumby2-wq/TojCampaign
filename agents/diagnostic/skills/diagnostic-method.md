# The Diagnostic Method — the $999 Model

The repeatable motion that turns a 45-minute discovery call into a report and an implementation offer. Worst case, the client gets a 45-min interview that names their bottlenecks. Best case, they reclaim 5-10 hours a week and buy the build.

## The promise

> 3-7 AI tools to implement that help the business reclaim **5-10 hours a week**.
> Worst case: a 45-min interview. Best case: 5-10 hours back a week.

## The three phases

### Phase 1 — Discovery (probe, don't prescribe)
An AI notetaker runs on the call. You ask probing questions and shut up. **The single biggest rule: do not prescribe on the first call. The first call is only for probing.**

The probing questions:
- "Walk me through your day yesterday — what do you typically do during a business day?"
- "What are the tasks in your business that you dread doing?"
- "Where does your work pile up?"
- "What have you tried to automate in the past that failed?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and delete any one process, what would it be?"

Capture frequency and duration for every pain ("how often" × "how long each") — that is the raw material for the financial-impact math later.

### Phase 2 — AI Analysis
Feed the full transcript to Claude. Prompt: identify 5-7 opportunities. Claude catches patterns across the whole call that a human misses. Then the operator reviews and adds picks from their own toolkit.

The working prompt shape:
> "I just had a call with a business owner. Here's the transcript. Find SaaS or AI products that address the client's pain points."

Tool research uses the AI-tool directories as the verification layer — **futurepedia.io** and **theresanaiforthat.com**. Most clients need basic SaaS tools; custom businesses need Claude skills / a custom GPT trained on their specific workflows.

### Phase 3 — The Report
Built in a Claude Design template. Six parts:
1. Executive summary (top 3)
2. Priority matrix (effort vs impact)
3. Tool recommendations
4. 4-day quick-start plan
5. Financial impact (KPI + ROI)
6. What comes after quick wins (the high-impact / high-effort projects)

Then the **review call**: screen-share the report, walk each recommendation, and close with three questions:
- Most urgent for you?
- DIY or want help?
- Your timeline?

~60% want implementation. Email the report after the call regardless.

## The qualification bar

Every recommendation must pull **at least one of three levers** (see `roi-levers`). If it pulls none, cut it.

## Why this works

The diagnostic is low-risk for the client (worst case, a free-feeling 45-min interview) and high-signal for you (a transcript is all Claude needs to produce a paid-grade report). It creates the proof and the pain-awareness that make the implementation tiers an easy yes.
