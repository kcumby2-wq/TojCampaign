# Skill: Three directions for a film

The producer has a film started from the kit: a format (a 60–90 second hero film, a 15–30 second cutdown, a sit-down, a quote card, the Same Drill, In Their Own Words, On the Mic), a lane, the athlete ID and, usually, a one-sentence story. Before the board is built, give three different ways to make it.

## What makes three real directions

Each is a different way in, not the same film three ways dressed:

- **The film the story suggests.** The one-sentence story, told plainly, in the format's length.
- **Another way in.** Start somewhere else: at the end, on a detail, on the place, on what someone else sees.
- **The bold one.** A stronger form the kit allows: a single take, the Same Drill match cut, letters on screen, the room's sound only.

Size each to the format: a hero film is 5–8 beats; a cutdown 3–4; a sit-down follows the conversation. Name each direction in a few words.

## Each direction has

- **A logline**: one sentence, the film it makes.
- **Beats**, in order: what we see, and what kind of line we hear. Describe the line to find; never write it.
- **Look**: light, colour, framing, pace. Use the kit's looks and say "(proposed)" for anything new.
- **Sound**: the speaker's voice, the room, music only if it's licensed (or none).
- **Three must-have shots**: specific and shootable in a day ("hands on the sled at first light", "the cones from above").
- **Why it fits** this film and this speaker.
- **Risks**: what could go wrong on the day, and the fallback.
- **Taste notes** it draws on: cite them as "Taste 12", with what to take. Take a lesson (the pace of the opening, how the title sits), never the shots themselves.

## The cards

Plan around what each release covers. A minor appears only wide and unidentifiable: hands, cleats, backs, a shadow. No faces, names, jersey numbers, schools or logos. A card that doesn't cover a face means no face in any direction. If a direction only works with something a card doesn't allow, it's not a direction.

## Recommend one

Say which you'd start with and why, in a sentence. Ask the producer at most three questions, only what they alone can answer (whether the archive clip exists, whether the field is open at dawn).
